# Involution Orbit-Harmonics Hilbert Series Statistic

## Task

Find a statistic `statistic(involution)` on involutions that returns a
**nonnegative integer** `istat_{n,a}(pi)` such that, for every `n >= 1` and every
`a` with `0 <= a <= n` and `a congruent to n (mod 2)`,

```text
H_{n,a}(q) = sum_{pi in M_{n,a}} q^{istat_{n,a}(pi)},
```

where `M_{n,a} = { pi in S_n : pi^2 = id, fix(pi) = a }` is the set of involutions
with exactly `a` fixed points and

```text
H_{n,a}(q) = Hilb(R(M_{n,a}); q)
```

is the Hilbert series of the graded ring obtained by applying **orbit harmonics**
to the corresponding permutation-matrix locus.

This `q_statistic_discovery` task has **no** public statistic. The single graded
exponent is the whole task, so `metadata.json` carries
`known_statistics: []` and there is no `known_statistic.py`. The fiber index
`(n, a)` is not submitted; it is read from the object as `(pi.n, pi.fix)`. The
checker groups the objects by fiber and compares the resulting
`q`-polynomial.

### Mathematical background

Let `X` be a finite point set in `C^N` and `I(X)` its vanishing ideal. Orbit
harmonics replaces `I(X)` by the homogeneous ideal `gr I(X)` generated by the
top-degree components `tau(f)` of the elements `f` of `I(X)`, and studies

```text
R(X) = C[x_1, ..., x_N] / gr I(X),
```

which is graded, and which is isomorphic to the ungraded space `C[X]` of
functions on `X`. Concretely, if `V_d` is the space of functions on `X` realized
by polynomials of degree at most `d`, then

```text
dim R(X)_d = dim V_d - dim V_{d-1},        C = V_0 subset V_1 subset ... = C[X],
```

so `Hilb(R(X); q)` is a `q`-analogue of `|X|` recording how much degree each
function on `X` needs. Here `N = n^2`, the coordinates are the entries `x_{ij}` of
an `n x n` matrix, and `X = M_{n,a}` is the set of permutation matrices of the
involutions with `a` fixed points. Since conjugation permutes `M_{n,a}`, the
quotient `R(M_{n,a})` is a graded `S_n`-module whose ungraded character is the
conjugation action on `M_{n,a}`; in particular

```text
H_{n,a}(1) = |M_{n,a}| = C(n, a) (n - a - 1)!!.
```

Liu, Ma, Rhoades and Zhu introduced these rings and gave a signed formula for
`grFrob(R(M_{n,a}); q)`; Zhu turned it into two positive combinatorial formulae,
and states as the next step exactly the problem posed here -- to find a linear
basis of `R(M_{n,a})` and "a statistic `stat : M_{n,a} -> Z_{>=0}` to interpret
the Hilbert series".

**The one solved fiber.** For `a = 0` the objects are the fixed-point-free
involutions, equivalently the perfect matchings of `[n]` with `n` even, and
Liu--Ma--Rhoades--Zhu prove the graded refinement

```text
grFrob(R(M_{n,0}); q) = sum_{lambda |- n, lambda even} q^{(n - lambda_1)/2} s_lambda
```

of the plethysm `h_{n/2}[h_2]`, where `lambda even` means every part of `lambda`
is even. Taking dimensions and conjugating the shapes -- an involution has RSK
shape with `fix(pi)` columns of odd length, so a fixed-point-free involution has a
shape whose conjugate is even -- gives

```text
H_{n,0}(q) = sum_{pi in M_{n,0}} q^{(n - lds(pi))/2},
```

with `lds(pi)` the length of a longest decreasing subsequence of the one-line
word, equivalently the number of rows of the RSK shape. This is the fiber with the
longest-increasing-subsequence and Tracy--Widom connection: Baik and Rains
computed the limiting law of the longest monotone subsequences of a random
(fixed-point-free) involution, so up to renormalization and reversal
`H_{n,0}` converges to a Tracy--Widom distribution. The executable contract
requires the **distribution** of `(n - lds(pi))/2` on this fiber; it does not
force that value on each individual involution. A structural answer is expected
to recover the solved formula pointwise. This is the nontrivial solved family;
besides the elementary identity and single-transposition fibers below, the
general positive-fixed-point fibers remain open.

**Anchors the answer must satisfy**, all verified by the generator over the whole
public range:

```text
H_{n,a}(1) = |M_{n,a}|                          every fiber
H_{n,n}(q) = 1                        so        istat_{n,n}(id) = 0
H_{n,n-2}(q) = 1 + (C(n,2) - 1) q
H_{n,0}(q) = sum_pi q^{(n - lds(pi))/2}         the solved fiber
deg H_{n,a} = (n - a)/2, except n/2 - 1 for a = 0
```

The transposition fiber `a = n - 2` is worth reading twice. Restricted to that
locus the variable `x_{ij}` is the indicator function of the single point `(i j)`,
so `V_1` is already all of `C[M_{n,n-2}]` and the series is `1 + (C(n,2) - 1) q`.
So `istat` must send **exactly one** of the `C(n,2)` transpositions to `0` and all
the others to `1`: no conjugation-invariant statistic can work, and the answer is
not a function of the RSK shape either -- the transpositions of `S_4` occupy only
the two shapes `(3, 1)` and `(2, 1, 1)`, with three objects each, while
`H_{4,2}(q) = 1 + 5q`. Nor does the solved formula extend
verbatim: `n - lds(pi)` is odd for `pi = (1 3)` in `S_4`, so `(n - lds)/2` is not
even an integer off the fixed-point-free fiber.

Two further regularities hold across the whole public range and are asserted in
the tests, but are recorded here as properties of the shipped data rather than
claimed as theorems:

```text
H_{2m,0}(q) = H_{2m-1,1}(q)
H_{n,a+2}(q) = H_{n,a}(q) with its top coefficient deleted, for a >= 3
```

## Objects

An **involution** is a permutation `pi in S_n` with `pi^2 = id`; equivalently a
partial matching of `[n]`, its `(n - a)/2` 2-cycles, together with its `a` fixed
points. The benchmark **object** is a single involution, and the fiber it is graded
in is the pair `(n, a) = (pi.n, pi.fix)`, both read off the object.

The canonical encoding is one-line notation: the comma-separated images
`pi(1), ..., pi(n)`. For example

```text
2,1,3,5,4
```

is `(1 2)(4 5)` in `S_5`, which lies in `M_{5,1}`, and the single `n = 1` object is
`1`. There are `C(n, a) (n - a - 1)!!` objects in the fiber `M_{n,a}` and
`|I_n| = sum_a |M_{n,a}|` objects of size `n` in all (`1, 2, 4, 10, 26, 76, ...`).

The Python submission receives an `Involution` object with:

- `involution.n`, `involution.size` (`= n`)
- `involution.images` (the tuple `(pi(1), ..., pi(n))`) and `involution(i)`
- `involution.fix` (`= a`, the fiber index) and `involution.fixed_points`
- `involution.pairs` (the 2-cycles as pairs `(i, pi(i))` with `i < pi(i)`)
- `involution.rsk_shape`, `involution.longest_increasing()`,
  `involution.longest_decreasing()`

The RSK shape and the two subsequence lengths are exposed because they are the
answer on the fixed-point-free fiber, not because they are graded variables of the
target. They are cheap on the large probes -- `O(n log n)` for the lengths and one
Schensted insertion pass for the shape -- so a genuine statistic can use them
freely.

## Public Statistics

None. The single graded exponent belongs to the discovery task, so
`metadata.json` carries `known_statistics: []` and there is no
`known_statistic.py`. What is public instead is the list of identities above,
every one of which the generator verifies over the whole public range.

## Public Data

`data/polynomials.json` contains, for every fiber `M_{n,a}` with `1 <= n <= 10`
(all 35 of them), the Hilbert series `H_{n,a}(q)`. Each case lists its `n` and `a`
and gives terms

```json
[q_exponent, coefficient]
```

so `H_{n,a}(q) = sum coefficient * q^{q_exponent}`, with zero coefficients omitted
and terms sorted by exponent. Every coefficient is positive, the constant term is
`1` (the degree-0 piece is the constants), the coefficients of one case total
`|M_{n,a}|`, and the degree is as listed among the anchors. The public range holds
`13231` objects in all.

`data/q_equals_1.json` contains the `q = 1` specialization: for each fiber the
single term `[a, |M_{n,a}|]`. Unlike the other problems this marginal is **not a
hint**: `H_{n,a}(1) = |M_{n,a}|` is just the ungraded isomorphism
`R(M_{n,a}) = C[M_{n,a}]`, which the fiber realizes whatever the submitted
statistic is. It is kept as a structural check on the object model -- that every
object of a case really has `fix(pi) = a` and that the case has the right size --
and it is checked from the same enumeration pass.

`data/instances.json` lists the public objects (`1 <= n <= 9`) as one `entries`
list of encodings per fiber. It carries no statistic values, because there are
none to publish.

The targets are computed straight from the definition above: the public generator
builds the filtration `V_0 subset V_1 subset ...` of `C[M_{n,a}]` by the degree
needed to realize a function and reads off the successive rank differences. Its
fast path requires agreement modulo two primes. Independently,
`certify_exact.py` computes every cumulative rank for `1 <= n <= 10` by a
proof-enabled sparse row echelon form over the rational field. The resulting
`data/exact_certificate.json` records every exact rank and the SHA-256 digest of
the scored polynomial file. Thus no public target relies only on probabilistic
modular agreement.

The shipped data is additionally cross-checked in
`tests/problems/test_involution_problem.py`: through `n <= 5` the filtration is
rebuilt over `Q` from *unreduced* partial-permutation monomials (and, for
`n <= 3`, from every monomial of the matrix variables, with no reduction at all), the
fixed-point-free fibers are recomputed as the longest-decreasing-subsequence
distribution of the enumerated fiber, and the same rebuilt filtration applied to
the *full* permutation matrix locus is required to reproduce Rhoades' answer
`sum_{w in S_n} q^{n - lis(w)}`.

**`data/instances.json` is a sample, not the scored set.** It lists 3,735 objects over 29 of the 35 scored fibers, while scoring runs over all 13,231 objects of the range in `data/polynomials.json`. Its `instances_max_*` field bounds that sample only. Enumerate the full scored range with `qtbench.combinatorics` before trusting a proposal.

## Submission

Submit a short, self-contained Python file defining:

```python
def statistic(involution) -> int:
    ...
```

The return value must be a nonnegative `int`. See `docs/checker.md` for the
admission checks; `examples/involution_moved_lds_submission.py` is a signature
template (the fixed-point-free answer applied to the non-fixed letters, which is
correct on the `a = 0` and `a = n` fibers and wrong on every fiber in between).

To score it:

```bash
uv run --frozen python scripts/evaluate/evaluate_scored_submission.py involution path/to/submission.py
```

## Scoring

After the capability and source-economy screens, the evaluator enumerates every public
fiber `M_{n,a}` once, groups the objects by the submitted exponent, and checks the
resulting `q`-polynomial against `data/polynomials.json`, together with the
`q = 1` fixed-point marginal. A mismatch stops the evaluation. Only a complete match
proceeds to the fresh-namespace shuffled replay and
large adversarial objects under CPU and memory limits. Every stage must pass,
though a pass is necessary but not sufficient for a genuine statistic (see
`docs/checker.md`).

For this problem, the resource gate is a useful practical lever. The fiber
`M_{n,a}` is easy to count, but the ranking cheat also needs `H_{n,a}(q)` itself.
Signed and positive formulas for the graded Frobenius series are known, as noted
above; the open problem is an objectwise statistic on the involutions, not the
mere existence of a target formula. No target computation documented here
scales to the size-1024 probes within the resource budget: the public
construction eliminates over a spanning set of the function space on an
exponentially large locus, whereas a proposed `istat` is expected to be a fast
function of a single involution.

## References
- Moxuan J. Liu, Yichen Ma, Brendon Rhoades, and Hai Zhu, "Involution matrix loci
  and orbit harmonics", Mathematische Zeitschrift (2025),
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-025-03736-4, arXiv:2409.06175, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06175.
  Introduces the rings `R(M_{n,a})`, gives a
  signed formula for the graded Frobenius image, proves the fixed-point-free
  graded refinement of `h_{n/2}[h_2]` used as the solved anchor above, and points
  out that the Hilbert series of the perfect-matching locus is the longest
  decreasing subsequence distribution and hence a Tracy--Widom limit.
- Hai Zhu, "Positive combinatorial formulae for involution matrix loci and orbit
  harmonics", arXiv:2507.11747, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11747. Two positive
  formulae for `grFrob(R(M_{n,a}); q)`, and the paper that explicitly poses the
  problem of a linear basis for `R(M_{n,a})` and a statistic interpreting its
  Hilbert series.
- Brendon Rhoades, "Increasing subsequences, matrix loci, and Viennot shadows",
  Forum of Mathematics Sigma 12 (2024), e97, arXiv:2306.08718, https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08718.
  The same question for the *full* permutation matrix locus, solved: a standard
  monomial basis from Viennot's shadow lines, giving
  `Hilb(R(S_n); q) = sum_{w in S_n} q^{n - lis(w)}`. This is the model answer the
  involution problem is missing, and the identity the tests use to validate the
  generator's linear algebra on a locus this problem never uses.
- Jinho Baik and Eric M. Rains, "The asymptotics of monotone subsequences of
  involutions", Duke Mathematical Journal 109 (2001), 205-281,
  arXiv:math/9905084, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9905084. The limiting distributions of the longest
  monotone subsequences of a random involution with or without a condition on the
  number of fixed points: Tracy--Widom laws for GOE, GUE and GSE and
  interpolations between them. This is what the `a = 0` Hilbert series converges
  to, and what an answer for general `a` would grade.
- Hai Zhu, "Plethysm and orbit harmonics", arXiv:2602.12623,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12623. The same orbit-harmonics programme for
  unordered set partition loci, where the graded refinement is of a general
  plethysm `h_k[h_j]`.
