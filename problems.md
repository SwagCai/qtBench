Registry status is `calibration` for problem `1` and `active` for the other 29 problems.

The catalogue below gives the mathematical content of each problem and notes
the distinctions that matter for evaluation.

- id `1`, `nc_area_qt_narayana_second_stat`: find a t-statistic on noncrossing partitions which, together with `area`, realizes the q,t-Narayana polynomials. This problem is already solved and is included as an easy test.
- id `2`, `dyck_area_bounce_exchange`: construct a bijection on Dyck paths that exchanges `area` and `bounce`, giving a combinatorial proof of q,t-Catalan symmetry.
- id `3`, `type_b_area_qt_catalan_second_stat`: find a statistic paired with `area` that realizes the type B q,t-Catalan polynomials.
- id `4`, `polyomino_area_bounce_exchange`: construct a bijection on parallelogram polyominoes that exchanges `area` and `bounce`. This gives a combinatorial proof of q,t-Narayana symmetry, the Narayana refinement of problem `2`.
- id `5`, `polyomino_area_bounce_transpose`: construct a bijection from polyominoes in an `m x n` box to those in an `n x m` box, preserving both `area` and `bounce`. Such a map proves the m,n symmetry of the q,t-Narayana polynomials.
- id `6`, `lpp_area_qt_theta_second_stat`: find the t-statistic paired with `area` on standardly labelled parallelogram polyominoes whose distribution is the Theta-operator Hilbert series `<Theta_{e_{m-1}} Theta_{e_{n-1}} e_1, e_{1^{m+n-1}}>`. This is Problem 7.13 of arXiv:2202.05706.
- id `7`, `ddyck_area_qt_unified_delta_second_stat`: find the t-statistic paired with `area` on standardly labelled doubly decorated Dyck paths whose distribution is the unified-Delta Hilbert series `<Theta_{e_k} Theta_{e_l} nabla e_{n-k-l}, e_{1^n}>`, as predicted in arXiv:2312.03956.
- id `8`, `uig_ginv_shareshian_wachs_q_stat`: give a composition-valued witness on Dyck graphs (natural unit interval graphs) and their `G`-nondescent permutations. Here `ginv` counts the inversions of the permutation whose two values are joined by an edge of `G`; this statistic is already public and supplies the exponent of `q`. The submission assigns a composition `theta` to each object, and sorting its parts gives a partition `lambda(theta)` that selects the elementary-basis term `e_lambda`. Grouping objects jointly by `ginv` and `lambda(theta)` must reproduce the coefficients `q^k e_lambda` in the Shareshian-Wachs chromatic quasisymmetric function `chi_G[X;q]`. In this concrete sense, `theta` is an object-by-object witness for the e-positive expansion. This is a q-statistic task in the benchmark taxonomy; passing the checker verifies the scored public cases, not the conjecture in full generality.
- id `9`, `ttree_inv_qt_xi_second_stat`: find the t-statistic paired with `inv` on standard zero-rooted tiered trees whose distribution is the Xi-operator Hilbert series `<Xi e_mu, e_{1^n}>`. This is the Symmetric Theta Conjecture and part of Problem 6.5 in arXiv:2202.05706.
- id `10`, `mld_area_qt_super_nabla_second_stat`: find the t-statistic paired with `area` on standard multi-labelled `k^n` Dyck paths whose distribution is the super-nabla Hilbert series `<nabla_*^k e_n, e_{1^n} (x) ... (x) e_{1^n}>`. This is the open statistic problem from arXiv:2303.00560; when `k = 1`, its specializations include the shuffle theorem and the valley Delta conjecture.
- id `11`, `lrp_area_qt_rectangular_delta_second_stat`: find the t-statistic paired with decorated `area` on standardly labelled rise-decorated rectangular paths whose distribution is the rectangular Delta Hilbert series `<([m+k]_q/[gcd(m,n)]_q) Theta_{e_k} p_{m,n}, h_{1^{n+k}}>`, from arXiv:2206.00131. The target is not q,t-symmetric, and the roles of `q` and `t` are transposed relative to the paper.
- id `12`, `tamari_park_trivariate_third_stat`: find a third statistic on pairs consisting of a parking function and a Tamari-smaller Dyck path. Together with the public statistics `chain` and `dinv`, it must realize qtBench's unrefined Hilbert-series shadow of Equation (49) of arXiv:1105.3738. The executable target forgets the fundamental-quasisymmetric factor indexed by `co(f)`, so passing it does not solve the full cited Frobenius problem. This is the only problem with two public statistics and a three-variable target.
- id `13`, `syt_qt_kostka_macdonald_pair_stat`: assign both statistics to pairs `(mu, T)`, where `T` is a standard Young tableau, so that their distribution is the modified Kostka-Macdonald polynomial `K~_{lambda mu}(q,t) = <H~_mu, s_lambda>`. This is Macdonald's original problem. Assaf's dual-equivalence graphs give a positive combinatorial formula for these Schur coefficients; the open task here is the narrower one of assigning a direct `(q,t)`-statistic pair to every tableau. Since neither statistic is public, the submission returns both exponents.
- id `14`, `uig_syt_llt_schur_q_stat`: find a statistic on pairs consisting of a Dyck graph and a standard Young tableau that realizes the Schur coefficients of the unicellular LLT polynomial `LLT_G[X;q]`. No statistic is supplied. Grojnowski and Haiman proved Schur positivity, but no general combinatorial rule is known. The specialization `LLT_G[X;1] = p_1^n` determines the objects and their number, making this a one-variable counterpart of problem `13`.
- id `15`, `inv_orbit_harmonics_hilbert_q_stat`: find a statistic on involutions in `S_n` with `a` fixed points whose distribution is the Hilbert series `H_{n,a}(q)` of the orbit-harmonics quotient `R(M_{n,a})` for the involution matrix locus. No statistic is supplied. The quotient is due to Liu-Ma-Rhoades-Zhu (arXiv:2409.06175), and Zhu poses the statistic problem in arXiv:2507.11747. The fixed-point-free fiber satisfies `H_{n,0}(q) = sum_pi q^{(n - lds(pi))/2}` and has the Baik-Rains Tracy-Widom limit. The identity and single-transposition fibers are elementary; the general fibers with fixed points remain open.
- id `16`, `match_jack_connection_q_stat`: find a statistic on perfect matchings of `{1..n} u {1h..nh}`, together with a partition `lambda`, that realizes the Goulden-Jackson Jack connection coefficients `c^lambda_{pi,sigma}(beta)`. No statistic is supplied, and the benchmark writes `q` for `beta`. This is the Matchings-Jack conjecture, open since 1996. The statistic must vanish exactly on bipartite matchings, giving the class algebra at `q = 0` and the double coset algebra at `q = 1`. The conjecture is known only for restricted families of `lambda, pi, sigma`.
- id `17`, `perm_q_eulerian_gamma_q_stat`: this is a qtBench-proposed strengthening of Han-Jouhet-Zeng's request for a combinatorial interpretation of `a_{n,k}(q)`, not the exact objectwise problem stated in their paper. It asks for a statistic on the Foata-Schuetzenberger permutations with `k-1` descents, no double descent, and no final descent whose distribution gives `a_{n,k}(q)` in the expansion `A_n(t,q) = sum_k a_{n,k}(q) t^{k-1} (-tq^k;q)_{n+1-2k}` of Carlitz's q-Eulerian polynomial. At `q = 1`, those objects count the classical Eulerian gamma-coefficients. This is the only problem based on Eulerian rather than Macdonald or Catalan polynomials.
- id `18`, `gpf_sel_ut_delta_xi_second_stat`: find the t-statistic paired with the selected-cell count `#S` on a gamma-parking function `p` and a subset `S` of its area cells. Their joint distribution must give the elementary coefficients of `Delta_{m_gamma} Xi e_lambda` after `q = 1 + u`, as in Conjecture 13.2 of arXiv:2203.10342. The known statistic is local, and the objects generalize classical parking functions (`gamma` empty and `lambda = 1^n`). The grading is by `u = q - 1`: e-positivity is conjectured after this shift without specializing `t`. The e-composition `eta(p)` read from the object supplies the partition grading.
- id `19`, `rtt_inv_qt_theta_second_stat`: find the t-statistic paired with `inv` on standard rooted tiered trees whose distribution is `<Theta_{e_mu} e_1, e_{1^{|mu|+1}}>`, the first identity in Problem 6.5 of arXiv:2202.05706. This is not problem `9`: there the root has the extra label `0` and is compatible with every vertex; here it has an ordinary label and participates in the compatibility relation. Consequently the fibers differ: `|stRTT(1,1,1)| = 60`, whereas `|RTT_0(1,1,1)| = 39`. In the source paper, the `t = 1` specialization is a theorem rather than a conjecture.
- id `20`, `lgpf_sel_ut_delta_xi_schur_second_stat`: the Schur-input companion to problem `18`. Find the t-statistic paired with `#S` on a lattice gamma-parking function of content `lambda'` and a subset of its area cells. The distribution must give the elementary coefficients of `Delta_{m_gamma} Xi s_lambda` at `q = 1 + u`. Theorem 1.2 of arXiv:2203.10342 proves the `t = 1` case, and the paper poses the unspecialized problem. The lattice-word condition is a global tableau-like constraint not present in problem `18`.
- id `21`, `tgt_inv_qt_ehrhart_second_stat`: find the t-statistic paired with `inv` on spanning trees of connected threshold graphs whose distribution is the `(q,t)`-Ehrhart function `Ehr_{q,t}(F_G(-n,1,...,1))` of the graph's flow polytope. Liu-Meszaros-Morales pose the problem in Section 6.1 of arXiv:1610.08370 and conjecture the required q,t-positivity. The `t = 1` marginal is a theorem; for the complete graph, the target is the bigraded Hilbert series of diagonal harmonics, recovering the classical spanning-tree model. The graph is part of the object, and the statistic cannot depend on the labelled tree alone.
- id `22`, `syt_promotion_csp_q_stat`: seek an intrinsic statistic on standard Young tableaux of rectangular or staircase shape whose distribution is the least-degree cyclic-sieving polynomial `C_lambda(q)` for Schuetzenberger promotion. Rectangles are solved by Rhoades' theorem, with `(maj - n(lambda)) mod N`; staircases are Problem 1.1 of Pon-Wang (arXiv:1003.2728), open since 2010. Because orbit position reconstructs the target tautologically and cheaply, the ordinary automatic score is a mechanical result; subsequent semantic review must assess whether the proposal is intrinsic.
- id `23`, `nc_q_kreweras_q_stat`: this is a qtBench-proposed natural-statistic challenge on noncrossing partitions, not a source-verified open problem. It asks for a statistic realizing the type A q-Kreweras numbers `Krew_lambda(q)` of Reiner-Sommers (arXiv:1605.09172), graded by block type. Summing over block types gives the MacMahon q-Catalan number; the known cyclic-sieving result is an identity at roots of unity, not an objectwise statistic.
- id `24`, `macdonald_fillings_inv_maj_exchange`: construct a bijection from standard HHL fillings of a non-hook partition shape `mu` to standard fillings of its conjugate `mu'`, exchanging the public `inv` and `maj` statistics. Gillespie's Problem 1.15 asks for the general content-preserving map; full bijections are known for hooks and only specializations or restricted families beyond them. This differs from problem `13`, whose objects are standard Young tableaux and whose two statistics are both unknown.
- id `25`, `parking_area_dinv_exchange`: construct a bijection on classical parking functions exchanging `area` and `dinv`, giving a direct combinatorial proof of the q,t-symmetry of the diagonal-coinvariant Hilbert series. McCammond--Thomas--Williams call the stronger involution problem long-standing and still wide open even for the alternating subspace. Unlike problem `2`, these are labelled parking functions and the target is not the q,t-Catalan polynomial.
- id `26`, `graph_sibling_tuft_exchange`: construct a vertex-count- and reduction-preserving bijection on unlabeled simple connected graphs exchanging the maximum closed-neighborhood sibling-class size minus one with the maximum number of leaves at one vertex. Fürnsinn--Gangl--Rubey prove the refined symmetry enumeratively and explicitly leave such an involution open. This is the benchmark's only problem on arbitrary graph isomorphism types.
- id `27`, `asm_dpp_weight_q_stat`: find a statistic on `n` by `n` alternating sign matrices whose q-enumerator is the sum-of-parts enumerator of descending plane partitions with largest part at most `n`. This is Stanton's Open Problem 1.4 and is the benchmark's first alternating-sign-matrix problem.
- id `28`, `shifted_setvalued_pq_weight_bijection`: construct Chiu--Marberg's Corollary 4.1 weight-preserving bijection between signed disjoint unions of set-valued shifted P- and Q-tableaux. The source explicitly leaves the map open beyond one-row shapes and notes the difficulty already at `mu = (3,1)`. This is the benchmark's first shifted and K-theoretic tableau problem.
- id `29`, `andrews_bressoud_successive_rank_bijection`: construct a weight-preserving bijection between ordinary partitions with bounded successive ranks and partitions avoiding three residue classes. The general Andrews--Bressoud theorem has no bijective proof; the public parameters exclude the solved Rogers--Ramanujan cases.
- id `30`, `improper_partition_matrix_inversion_sequence_bijection`: construct the semi-weight/distinct-entry preserving bijection in Chern--Fu Question 5.5 from improper partition matrices in the minus class to restricted inversion sequences. This is the benchmark's first partition-matrix and inversion-sequence problem.

The file `data/instances.json` is usually an illustrative sample; problem `26`
publishes its complete 996-object scored corpus there. Together these files
contain 133,736 objects, whereas scoring evaluates 5,695,870. The scored range
is specified by `data/polynomials.json` and `metadata.json`. Most target-emission
entry points are adjacent to their problem; problems `4` and `5` share
`scripts/generate/generate_polyomino_data.py`, and problem `3` re-emits committed
target records rather than independently deriving them. The complete reproduction
contract is in [`data_generation.md`](docs/data_generation.md). Proposals should be
tested on the scored range, not only on any smaller sample.

Each `metadata.json` gives the public range for its problem. Problem `1` includes
every `NC(n,k)` with `1 <= n <= 10`, while problem `23` includes every
noncrossing partition with `1 <= n <= 11`. After capability and source-economy
screening, the evaluator compares the resulting coefficients exactly with the
public targets. Only a numerical match reaches shuffled replay and the resource gate.
